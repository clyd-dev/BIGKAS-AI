1. The 60-second explanation

Say this first

"BIGKAS-AI helps a teacher assess how well a Grade 3 to 6 learner reads aloud, in English or Filipino. The teacher or the learner records the child reading a short passage. The system listens, compares what the child said with the printed passage word by word, and computes accuracy, reading speed and fluency, and the Phil-IRI reading level. A machine-learning model then suggests the learner's main reading weakness, and the system recommends activities for teacher and parent. The teacher always reviews the result and makes the final decision. It does not replace the teacher; it saves the manual tallying time and keeps a record of progress."

2. The problem and who it is for

The problem

In public elementary schools, a teacher listens to each learner read aloud, marks every mistake on paper, counts words and minutes by hand, and then looks up a reading level (the Phil-IRI procedure). This takes a lot of time per child, so many learners are assessed rarely, and the specific kind of reading difficulty a child has is often not recorded in a way that can be tracked over time.

Caution about statistics

The project README quotes an EDCOM 2 figure that "70% of Grade 3 learners struggle with basic phonemic awareness". Do not quote this number unless you can name the exact source document. If asked, say "national reports describe a serious reading-literacy gap" and cite the source you can actually show.

5. The full pipeline, start to finish

This is the journey of one reading assessment. Learn the ten steps in order. For each step: what happens, what the panel will see, and a sentence you can say.

1

The teacher chooses the learner and a reading passage

The teacher picks a learner, then the language (English or Filipino), then the type of assessment: oral reading only, comprehension only, or combined. The system offers passages that match the learner's grade. A passage from one grade above or below is allowed only if the teacher deliberately chooses that option. Passages are short (up to 50 words) and each has comprehension questions.

On screen: Assessments → New assessment.

"We follow the grade-level rule of Phil-IRI: the passage must match the learner's grade unless the teacher chooses otherwise."
2

The learner reads the passage aloud

Two ways. (a) The teacher records the learner on the teacher's own device. (b) The learner opens their own student screen with a PIN, sees the passage, reads it, and answers the questions; the teacher can watch the live status. The microphone recording stops by itself at 3 minutes.

On screen: record button, timer, sound-level bar; or the student's reading screen with a big passage.

"The child reads normally. Nothing special is needed other than a microphone and a reasonably quiet place."
3

The recording is saved

The voice recording is stored on the school's server first, before any analysis. If anything fails later, the recording is not lost and the teacher can press "Analyze" again without making the child read again. The teacher can also replay it to check the AI's work.

"We save the recording first so a technical problem never costs the child another reading."
4

The Listener turns the voice into words

Speech recognition writes down what was said, with the time each word started and ended. It is set up to keep hesitations and repeated words instead of "tidying them up", because those are exactly what a reading assessment needs to see. Before this, the system also measures the recording itself: how much of it was speech, how noisy the background was, and whether any sound was not the child reading. If nobody is speaking, it stops and says so instead of guessing words.

On screen: Results → Recording quality ("Clear recording", "Some background noise", "Noisy recording", "No voice heard").

"The system checks the recording quality and tells the teacher when the result may be unreliable because of noise."
5

The Marker compares what was said with the passage

The words spoken are lined up against the printed passage in order. Each word is labelled the way a reading teacher would:

  • Correct: read as written.
  • Mispronunciation: a different word that sounds like the target.
  • Substitution: a different, unrelated word.
  • Omission: a word skipped. Not reached: the child stopped before the end.
  • Insertion: an extra word added.
  • Repetition or false start (for example "ma-mabilis").
  • Self-correction: a mistake the child fixed immediately (not counted as an error).
  • Off-passage: talking that is not reading (not scored as reading errors).

On screen: Results → word-by-word comparison with colours.

"This is the same thing a teacher does with a red pen, but automatically and consistently."
6

The system computes the reading measures

MeasurePlain meaning
Accuracy %Words read correctly divided by words in the passage.
Words per minute (WPM)Correct words divided by the recording length, scaled to one minute. Compared with a benchmark for the learner's grade.
Fluency (0 to 10)How smooth the reading was: starts at 10 and loses points for long pauses and for very slow or rushed pace.
Prosody (0 to 10)An estimate of expression and rhythm (see the honest limits section: this one is an estimate).
Phil-IRI levelAccuracy 97% and above is Independent; 90 to 96% is Instructional; below 90% is Frustration. Comprehension has its own cut-offs: 80% and above, 59 to 79%, and 58% and below.
"The reading level uses the official Phil-IRI cut-offs, not our own numbers, so the printed DepEd forms always follow the DepEd rule."
7

The Pattern Spotter suggests the main weakness

Twelve measurements (accuracy, speed, fluency, error rates, pause rate, and so on) are sent to a trained machine-learning model. It answers with one of five categories and how sure it is:

#CategoryMeaning
0Independent reader / no weaknessReads accurately and smoothly.
1Phonemic awarenessTrouble linking letters to sounds; errors that sound like the target word.
2Decoding accuracyReads words incorrectly; many wrong words.
3Oral reading fluencyReads slowly or haltingly, many pauses.
4Reading comprehensionUnderstanding the text. Measured by the comprehension questions, not by the voice.
"The model suggests; it does not diagnose. It was trained on 519 voice clips of Grade 3 and 4 learners that teachers had reviewed and labelled."
8

The Double-Checker tests the suggestion against the evidence

A suggestion is only saved if the measured evidence supports it. Example: if the model says "phonemic awareness" but the child made only one such slip, the system does not store that weakness. It picks the next most likely category that the evidence does support, or stores none. The system also raises plain-language warnings, for example "recording is very short", "speech recognition was unsure of many words", "accuracy is unusually low, which often means a poor recording". If the AI service is off, a simple rule gives an estimate and the result is clearly labelled as rule-based.

On screen: Results → "How this result was produced" and the findings list.

"We built in checks so that the system tells the teacher when not to trust it."
9

The teacher reviews and decides

The teacher sees the result, the explanation of why the level was assigned, and the recording. Then they choose one of three decisions: Accept, Override (change the level or weakness, with a written reason, or re-score by hand), or Invalidate (discard a bad recording, with a reason). The AI's original numbers are always kept next to the teacher's decision, so there is a clear record of who decided what.

On screen: Results → Teacher decision.

"BIGKAS advises, the teacher decides. Every change needs a reason, and it is recorded."
10

Action and tracking

The result drives what happens next: recommended activities for the weakness, assignment to the learner (and the parent for home activities), notifications to the parent, progress charts, class "needs attention" lists on the teacher dashboard, and printable DepEd forms (passage rating sheet, individual summary record, class reports, the school reading profile). Learners earn points, streaks and badges for practice.

On screen: Dashboard, Interventions, Learner progress, Reports, Parent dashboard, Student badges.

"The assessment is not the end. It triggers a plan and tracks whether the child improves."

7A. Explaining the results page (the "How to read this result" card)

The results page ends with a card that answers the panel's most likely question: "What do these numbers mean, and how did the AI get them?" You can scroll to it and read from it. It has three parts.

Part 1: how the result was produced (say it in six words each)

StepWhat the page says, in plain words
1. ListenThe recording is checked for speech versus noise, then the speech model writes down the words and when each was said.
2. CompareThe heard words are lined up against the passage. Each passage word is correct, mispronounced, substituted, skipped or not reached.
3. CountAccuracy, words per minute, pauses and the comprehension score are simple arithmetic. No AI in this step.
4. SuggestThe Random Forest looks at 12 rates together and suggests the one weakest skill. A suggestion, not a measurement.
5. CheckThe suggestion is kept only if the measured evidence supports it; otherwise the page says so.
6. You decideThe teacher accepts, overrides or invalidates. The AI's original values are kept beside the decision.

Part 2: what each number means

NumberWhat it isCut-offs / what to say
Word accuracyCorrect words divided by words in the passage. Counted by rules.97% and above independent; 90 to 96% instructional; below 90% frustration (Phil-IRI).
Words per minuteCorrectly read words divided by the length of the recording in minutes. Mistakes and long silences lower it.Compared with a grade benchmark. Speed alone is not a diagnosis.
ComprehensionQuestions answered correctly. Shown only when questions were asked.80% and above independent; 59 to 79% instructional; below 59% frustration.
Total errorsMispronunciations, substitutions, omissions, insertions, repetitions, words not reached.Self-corrections, hesitations and pauses are shown but not counted as errors.
Model confidenceHow strongly the model's trees agreed on the suggested skill.Not the chance that it is right. Say: "agreement, not accuracy".
Model accuracyHow often the model matched teachers on held-out clips in development (62.5%).About four in ten suggestions can disagree with a teacher, which is why the teacher confirms every result.

Part 3: the ten questions on the card

The card carries its own FAQ, so a panelist can read the answer straight off the screen. In short:

  • Does the AI decide the level? No. The level comes from counting against the Phil-IRI cut-offs. The AI only suggests the weakest skill.
  • Level versus weakness? The level says how hard the passage was for the learner. The weakness says which skill is most likely behind it.
  • "No significant weakness detected"? No skill was below its cut-off in this reading. It describes one reading of one passage, not a guarantee.
  • "Can't tell"? There was not enough evidence, usually comprehension without questions. The system will not guess.
  • Confident model but fine numbers? Trust the numbers and your own listening; confidence only means the trees agreed.
  • Word marked wrong but said correctly? The speech model can mishear. Words it was unsure about carry a wavy underline. Play the audio and correct it in the verdict.
  • WPM differs from a stopwatch? Only correct words count and the time is the whole recording.
  • Noisy audio or early stop? See "What to check before you decide" above it; re-assess or use the verdict.
  • Filipino and English equal? No. Training was Grades 3 and 4, more Filipino than English, with a stock speech model.
  • What happens to an override? The AI's values stay stored beside it; the teacher's decision is the record.

Tip: during the demo, open this card last. After showing the level and the weakness, scroll down and say: "Everything you just saw is explained here, including how far to trust the AI."

8. Honest limits (read before the defense)

Panelists respect a team that knows its limits. These are real and we should state them ourselves before being asked.

What is solid

  • Complete working workflow from recording to result to action.
  • Reading level uses the official Phil-IRI cut-offs.
  • Teacher always has the final say, and it is recorded.
  • The system warns when a result may be unreliable.
  • Works even when the AI model is off (clearly labelled fallback).
  • Privacy and security measures built in; 371 automated tests pass.

What is weak (say it plainly)

  • The classifier is 62.5% accurate on a 104-clip test set, and about 50% in cross-validation. It is useful, not definitive.
  • It was trained on Grade 3 and 4 only, from 36 learners, not Grades 5 and 6.
  • Only 3 clips of "comprehension" weakness existed, so the model cannot learn that category. Comprehension is measured by the questions instead.
  • The speech engine is the standard pre-trained one, not yet tuned for Filipino children's voices.
  • We have not yet measured the whole system end to end against a teacher's marking in real classrooms.
The honest one-liner

"It is a decision-support tool. The measurements (accuracy, speed, level) are computed directly and transparently. The weakness suggestion comes from a model trained on a small, first-version dataset, so we treat it as a suggestion that the teacher confirms. Our next step is more data, more grades, and a classroom validation."

9. FAQ the panel is likely to ask, with defense answers

Each answer is short enough to say aloud. The If pressed line is the follow-up if the panelist digs deeper. The Do not say line is a trap to avoid.

Q1What exactly does BIGKAS-AI do, in one sentence?

It listens to a learner read aloud in English or Filipino, scores the reading against the printed passage, determines the Phil-IRI level, suggests the main reading weakness, and recommends activities for the teacher and parent to act on.

Q2Why is this needed when Phil-IRI already exists?

We do not replace Phil-IRI; we follow it. The levels and the forms are DepEd's. What we remove is the manual tallying, and we add a consistent record, trend tracking, and a specific next step for each child. Paper Phil-IRI tells you the level; it rarely tells you what to do next or whether the child improved.

Q3Does it replace the teacher?

No. Every result goes to the teacher, who can accept it, override it with a written reason, or discard it. The AI's original numbers are kept next to the teacher's decision. The teacher is the assessor of record.

Q4How does it know what the child said?

It uses Whisper, a widely used open-source speech-recognition model, running on our own server. It gives the words and the time of each word. We configured it to keep repeated and hesitant words so the mistakes are not cleaned away, and we check the recording quality first.

If pressed Is it accurate on children's Filipino speech? — "It is the standard pre-trained model, not yet fine-tuned for our learners. That is why the system reports recording quality and speech-recognition confidence, lets the teacher replay the audio, and lets the teacher override. A fine-tuning script is prepared for when we have more data and a GPU."

Q5How accurate is the system?

Two different things. The measurements (accuracy %, WPM, level) are direct calculations; their reliability depends on how well the speech was recognized, and we show that. The weakness suggestion comes from a machine-learning model that agreed with the teachers' label on 62.5% of its 104 test clips across five categories. Always guessing the most common category would score about 41%, so the model adds real signal, but it is a suggestion, not a diagnosis.

If pressed Why not higher? — "Small first dataset (519 clips), five overlapping categories, labels that depend on human judgment, and a test set of only 104 clips. Its top-two suggestions contain the teacher's label about 86% of the time. More data is the main route to improving it."

Do not say "It is highly accurate", "it is 62.5% accurate at reading assessment", or "it diagnoses dyslexia/reading disorders". The 62.5% is only the weakness-category model on its test clips.

Q6What data did you train it on? Who labelled it?

519 usable voice clips of Grade 3 and Grade 4 learners reading scripted passages in Filipino and English. Each clip was annotated word by word (what was skipped, replaced, added, paused), measurements were computed from those annotations, and teachers reviewed and confirmed the weakness label for each clip.

If pressed How many learners? — "36 learners, several clips each. Most clips are Filipino (about 4 in 5)." Grade 5 and 6 are supported by the passages and screens but are not yet represented in the training clips.

Q7What happens if the AI or the internet is down?

The recording is saved first, so nothing is lost. If the AI classifier is unreachable, a simple rule-based estimate is used and the result is clearly labelled "rule-based, ML service offline". If no speech engine is available, the system refuses to score instead of showing fake results, and the teacher can press Analyze again later.

Q8Is the children's data safe?

Access is by role (a teacher sees their own class, a parent sees only their linked child). Student PINs and personal identity fields are stored encrypted. Logins are rate-limited and accounts lock after repeated failures. Passwords need to meet a strength policy. Email verification uses a one-time code. Every page is sent with security headers and is not cached after logout.

If pressed Data Privacy Act? — "We applied privacy-by-design practices: restricted access, encryption of identifying fields, audit logs. A formal consent workflow for parents is on our future-work list and a formal compliance review would be done together with the school and division before wide deployment."

Do not say "We are fully compliant with the Data Privacy Act" or "DepEd approved".

Q9Why only English and Filipino? What about Hiligaynon or other mother tongues?

Scope. The reading materials and the training clips are English and Filipino, which are the languages used for the Phil-IRI. The system is designed so more languages can be added when passages, recordings and labels exist for them.

Q10Which grades does it cover?

The system, passages and DepEd forms cover Grades 3 to 6, matching the Phil-IRI range. The current classifier was trained on Grade 3 and 4 recordings; extending it to Grades 5 and 6 is the first item of future work.

Q11How is the reading level decided?

By the official Phil-IRI word-reading cut-offs: 97% and above Independent, 90 to 96% Instructional, below 90% Frustration. When comprehension questions are taken, they are scored with the Phil-IRI comprehension cut-offs and the overall level is the lower of the two.

Q12How do you measure comprehension? The voice can't tell that.

Correct, and we do not claim it can. Comprehension is measured by questions the learner answers after reading, scored against the Phil-IRI cut-offs. The voice-based model is never the source of a comprehension finding.

Q13What if the room is noisy or the child is shy and speaks softly?

The system measures the recording itself and shows "clear", "some noise", "noisy" or "no voice heard", lists background sounds with times, and warns when speech recognition was unsure. For a poor recording the teacher can replay it, re-record, override the result, or invalidate it.

Q14Can a teacher disagree with the AI?

Yes, and that is by design. An override requires a stated reason, can include manual re-scoring, and the AI's original result stays on record. Parents and DepEd forms use the teacher's final decision.

Q15How do young learners log in safely?

With a personal 6-digit PIN issued by the teacher, on a separate kid-friendly login. The PIN is stored encrypted, expires, and attempts are rate-limited.

Q16What does the parent see?

Their own child's results in plain language, progress over time, activities to do at home, and a message thread with the teacher. They cannot see other children.

Q17What is new compared with existing reading apps?

Four things together: bilingual English and Filipino assessment aligned to Phil-IRI; official DepEd forms generated from the data; a teacher-in-the-loop decision record; and a speech engine that runs on the school's own server so there is no per-use cloud fee.

Q18What hardware or internet does it need?

An ordinary small server (a 4 GB virtual server was our target) with no special graphics card, and a browser with a microphone on the teacher's or learner's device. Speech analysis is slower on a CPU (seconds to minutes), which is why the screen shows progress and the recording is saved first.

Q19Did real teachers or learners test it?

Teachers validated the labels used for training and the recordings are from real learners. Be ready to describe exactly what testing or consultation your group actually did with teachers and learners.

Do not say anything about pilot testing or user feedback that you cannot back with a record. The project files do not contain a formal usability study, so only claim what you did.

Q20Why a Random Forest and not deep learning?

Our data is small (hundreds of clips) and tabular (12 measurements). That is where Random Forests work well, are fast on ordinary hardware, and can show which measurements mattered. Deep learning needs far more data and is harder to explain to teachers.

Q21What are the risks of mislabelling a child?

That is why the weakness is a suggestion, shown with its confidence and evidence, and always passes through the teacher. The system will not store a weakness that the measured evidence does not support, and an invalid recording can be discarded with a recorded reason.

Q22What will you do next / how will it improve?

  1. Collect more recordings, especially Grade 5 and 6 and more comprehension cases.
  2. Tune the speech engine on Filipino learners' recordings (the preparation scripts exist).
  3. Validate end to end against teachers' manual Phil-IRI marking in real classrooms.
  4. Add a parent consent workflow and a formal privacy review.

Q23Is it approved by DepEd?

No. It is a capstone prototype aligned to DepEd's Phil-IRI levels and forms. Adoption would require review and approval by the division and school authorities.

Q24The home page talks about DepEd Phil-IRI forms. Are they filled in automatically?

They are pre-filled and then reviewed by the teacher. The system prepares printable records from the assessment data: the Group Screening Test records (Forms 1A/1B, from scores the teacher enters or imports), the Grade Level Passage Rating Sheet (Form 3A/3B), the Individual Summary Record (Form 4), and the School Reading Profile (Form 2, for the administrator). Some fields are left for the teacher to complete by hand.

Do not say "everything on every form is filled in automatically".

Q25Is there a privacy policy?

Yes, a short Privacy Notice is linked in the footer of the home page (/privacy). It states what is collected, who can see it, how speech is processed and which safeguards exist. It also says plainly that parental consent and a retention period still have to be agreed with the school and division. It is a prototype notice, not a formal Data Privacy Act review.

Q26What does the confidence percentage mean? Is the AI 80% sure?

It is how strongly the model's decision trees agreed on the suggested skill. It is not the chance the suggestion is right. On held-out clips the model matched teachers 62.5% of the time, so the result is a suggestion for the teacher to confirm.

Do not say "the AI is 80% accurate" or "there is an 80% chance it is correct".

Q27Does the AI decide the reading level?

No. The level is counted: accuracy, and comprehension when questions were asked, against the Phil-IRI cut-offs. The AI only suggests which skill looks weakest, and the teacher accepts, overrides or invalidates it. The results page has a "How to read this result" card that says this on screen.

Q28The page says "Can't tell" for comprehension. Is that a bug?

No, it is deliberate. Comprehension can only be judged from the questions, not from how a voice sounds. If no questions were answered, the system says it cannot tell rather than guess.

Q29The words per minute is lower than what I timed. Why?

Only correctly read words are counted, and the time used is the whole recording. Silence at the start or end, long pauses and mistakes all lower it. It is a documented rule, and the teacher can re-score a result.

10. Suggested demo order and team roles

  1. (30 s) Home page and the two login paths (staff vs. learner PIN).
  2. (30 s) Admin: System status page showing the speech engine and the classifier online.
  3. (60 s) Teacher dashboard and starting a new assessment (choose learner, language, grade-matched passage).
  4. (60 s) Record a short reading. Make one deliberate mistake (skip a word) so the comparison has something to show.
  5. (90 s) Results page: level and why, word-by-word comparison, recording quality, weakness and evidence, recommended activities.
  6. (30 s) Teacher decision: accept or override with a reason.
  7. (45 s) Parent view of the same result and the student portal (badges).
  8. (30 s) DepEd form / report print.
Demo safety tips
  • Do a full rehearsal on the same device and network you will use. The first analysis after the server starts can be slow while the speech model loads.
  • Keep a pre-recorded, already-analysed assessment open in another tab as a backup in case the live analysis is slow.
  • Use a quiet spot and a headset microphone if possible.
  • If the live result looks odd, use it: show the warning panel and the teacher override. That demonstrates the safeguards.

11. Say / don't say cheat sheet

Say

  • "Decision support for the teacher."
  • "The teacher decides; every change has a reason."
  • "Aligned with Phil-IRI levels and forms."
  • "A first version trained on 519 teacher-validated clips."
  • "We know the limits and here is our plan."
  • "The system warns when a result may be unreliable."

Don't say

  • "It is very accurate" or "it is 100% reliable".
  • "It replaces teachers" or "it diagnoses".
  • "DepEd approved" or "fully Data-Privacy-Act compliant".
  • "Trained on Grades 3 to 6" (it was Grades 3 and 4).
